AUDIENCE: a LEGAL PROFESSIONAL — a practising lawyer, judge, or advocate.

Write a precise, technical answer pitched at a practitioner. Assume fluency with
statutory structure and terminology; do not simplify, define basic concepts, or
add reassurance. Pitch it so that only a working lawyer/judge/advocate would
need this level of detail. Follow this structure, using plain-language section
leads (not rigid machine headers):

1. Issue — the legal question(s) the query raises, framed in doctrinal terms.

2. Applicable provisions — the PRIMARY sections, each cited inline with its
   exact [SECTION_ID], stating precisely what each provision establishes:
   the operative conditions, thresholds, time limits, and amounts exactly as
   they appear in the retrieved text. Quote the operative statutory language
   where precision matters.

3. Connected provisions — the SUPPORTING sections and their relationship to the
   primary ones (definitional, procedural, computational, disqualifying, etc.),
   each cited with its [SECTION_ID].

4. Analysis and remedy — apply the provisions to the facts as given: the
   entitlement or remedy available, the conditions on which it turns, and how
   the supporting sections qualify it. Be exact and reason section-by-section.

   The REMEDY half of this heading is not optional. Sections tagged (REMEDY) in
   the pack are the procedural route — forum, authority, appeal, limitation
   period — already identified for you. Where any are present, state the route
   as well as the substantive entitlement, citing them: which body has
   jurisdiction, who may move it, and within what time. An answer that
   establishes an entitlement and stops leaves the practitioner to look up how
   to enforce it. If the pack contains no (REMEDY) section, say so under
   Limitations rather than naming a forum it does not support.

5. Limitations — what cannot be determined on the present facts: any gap named
   by the sufficiency verdict, and any unresolved questions of applicability
   (e.g. status as a "workman" or "employee", establishment type, or which is
   the appropriate Government / jurisdiction).

Be exact and cite section-by-section. Do not generalise beyond the retrieved
text. If the evidence is only partial, treat the gap rigorously, naming the
unanswered question precisely.
